Wholesale Traceability Data-Sharing Policy

Rules for externally shared Origin Passport and shipment traceability data
Policy ID: DG-TR-004  ·  Version: 2.1  ·  Effective: 2026-03-01
Owner: Data Governance Council  ·  Applies to: Sourcing, Production, Quality, Wholesale Operations, Engineering, Support

1. Purpose

This policy defines which Cocoarynth traceability information may be shared with wholesale partners, which information must remain internal, and which information requires separate approval. It supports transparent sourcing without exposing personal, commercially sensitive, or internal deliberative data.

2. Scope

The policy applies to Origin Passports, retailer integration files, shipment attachments, support responses, and other structured or unstructured disclosures of cacao-origin and production information to wholesale recipients. It applies regardless of transport format.

Minimum necessary rule: Share only fields needed for the approved wholesale purpose. A field being eligible for sharing does not require its inclusion in every export.

3. Data classifications

A. Approved traceability fields

The following fields may be shared for an approved traceability purpose when their values have passed the relevant operational review.

Field categoryExamplesConditions
Passport identityOrigin Passport ID, replacement or version referenceUse approved external identifiers.
Product and batchProduct name, finished batch number, cacao percentageMust match shipment and product records.
Cacao originCountry, growing region, producer or cooperative name, harvest yearUse the approved sourcing record and agreed producer naming.
CertificationCertification status applicable to the origin lotShare only current approved wording; do not imply certification beyond its scope.
Production and shipmentProduction date, shipment dateShare dates needed to connect the finished batch to its delivery.

B. Internal-only fields

The following information must not be included in wholesale traceability exports or routine retailer support responses.

Field categoryExamplesReason
Commercial termsNegotiated cacao prices, supplier rates, payment terms, marginsCommercially confidential.
Internal quality materialQuality notes, tasting deliberations, non-public defect assessmentsInternal operational analysis.
Employee collaborationEmployee comments, reviewer notes, internal messagesNot part of the approved traceability record.
Security and system dataCredentials, access tokens, private record keys, debugging outputSecurity-sensitive and unnecessary for the recipient.

C. Separate approval required

The following information may be disclosed only when Data Governance and the responsible business owner approve a documented purpose and channel.

Field categoryExamplesApproval considerations
Personal contact informationProducer phone number, personal email, home or precise personal addressConsent, purpose, minimization, recipient, retention.
Non-public commercial informationContract volumes, special terms, forward commitmentsContract restrictions and commercial owner approval.
Precise location dataFarm coordinates or household-level locationProducer safety, consent, and necessity.
Supporting documentsCertificates containing extra names or identifiersRedaction and permitted-use review.

4. Origin Passport baseline

A standard wholesale Origin Passport may contain the passport ID, product name, batch number, country, growing region, producer or cooperative name, harvest year, cacao percentage, certification status, production date, and shipment date. This baseline is compatible with the approved traceability classification above.

Supplier pricing, producer contact details, internal quality notes, and employee comments are not part of the baseline and must remain excluded.

5. Responsibilities

  • Data owners approve purposes and field classifications.
  • Sourcing and Quality approve the relevant origin and certification facts.
  • Wholesale Operations confirms that the recipient and shipment are authorized.
  • Engineering implements field allowlists and tests that prohibited fields are absent.
  • Support uses approved artifacts and avoids copying internal records into retailer responses.

6. Sharing controls

  1. Confirm the wholesale purpose and intended recipient.
  2. Select the approved schema or artifact for that purpose.
  3. Verify data against the governed source records.
  4. Check that no internal-only or unapproved fields are present.
  5. Use the approved transfer channel and retain the required issuance record.
  6. Escalate uncertain classifications before disclosure.

7. Corrections and incidents

Correct externally shared traceability information through the approved replacement process; do not silently alter an issued historical record. Suspected disclosure of internal-only, personal, commercial, or security information must be reported immediately through Cocoarynth's data incident process. Do not resend or further distribute the affected file while review is pending.

8. Interpretation

This policy determines whether information may be shared. A partner's integration requirements determine which permitted fields and formats are needed for a particular engagement. Product code or an issue tracker determines what the application currently implements. Those sources serve different purposes and must not be treated as interchangeable.
